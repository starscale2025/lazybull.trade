## 1. The Category We Own

**We don't sell "trading education." We sell "Visual Options" — options you can see, drag, and paper-trade before you risk a rupee.**

Why name a new shelf instead of fighting on the old one: the trading-education software market is ~$1.35B. Winning 2% is single-digit millions and a knife-fight with tastytrade and Varsity. So we refuse the "education" label and name our own shelf.

| Old shelf (crowded) | Our shelf (empty) |
|---|---|
| Options **analytics** (OptionStrat, Sensibull) — for people who already understand | **Visual Options** — the on-ramp *before* analytics makes sense |
| Options **courses** (tastytrade, Varsity) — watch, retain nothing | **Options you can see** — drag it, don't sit through it |
| Options **signals** (the Telegram guru) — "buy now," illegal, 91% lose | **Options you practice** — paper-only, no tips |

**The defensible claim (and its limit):** no competitor takes a true beginner from "what is a call" to a paper spread inside one visual surface with AI narration. OptionStrat visualizes for experts; tastytrade lectures; Sensibull is a dashboard for existing traders. This is built, not roadmapped — 14-chapter runnable primer + drag-to-build chain + AI explainer. **But "category" is a marketing frame, not a moat.** The actual moat is whatever an incumbent can't cheaply copy: the SEBI-compliant paper-only positioning + the India F&O wedge + speed of iteration. Say "we created Visual Options" externally; internally, know the moat is execution and compliance posture, both of which an incumbent *can* eventually bolt on. The defense is being first and being trusted, not the phrase.

**The phrase that travels:** *"Options You Can See."* Fits a hero, a 9-second TikTok, a broker deck. Use it relentlessly.

## 3. Messaging Hierarchy by Audience

Four audiences, four first sentences, one product. Don't lead every market with the same line.

### A. Beginner (global free funnel)
- **Promise:** "Understand options before you ever risk a dollar."
- **Emotion:** relief + curiosity.
- **Hook:** 14-chapter primer, every concept a runnable demo.
- **CTA:** *Start free — no card, no broker.*
- **Avoid:** anything that smells like "get rich." This cohort got burned by exactly that.

### B. Serious Retail / Prosumer (US/UK/CA/AU/SEA — the ARR)
- **Promise:** "Visualize, model, and stress-test any options trade — with an AI that explains the why."
- **Emotion:** competence + edge.
- **Hook:** drag-to-build + live Black-Scholes Greeks. Frame against the $50–150/mo stack they already pay (OptionStrat + Unusual Whales + TradingView).
- **CTA:** *Start your 7-day Pro trial.* Card-required — yes, this suppresses signups, but card-up trials convert far higher than opt-in; we accept fewer-but-qualified trials given we can't afford a long nurture funnel.
- **Honesty flag:** do **not** claim "replaces Unusual Whales" or "real-time data" until we have a live IV/flow feed. We have lagged/mock data today. Sell on *understanding*, not data parity. Overselling here is the fastest way to get torched in r/options.

### C. India F&O (the wedge — free)
- **Promise:** **"91% of F&O traders lose money. Learn why — on paper — before you risk a rupee."**
- **Emotion:** fear-of-loss → empowerment. Highest-converting attention hook in Indian fintech because the number is SEBI's, not ours.
- **Stat discipline:** SEBI study found ~91% of individual F&O traders made net losses; aggregate losses were ~₹1.05 lakh crore in FY25 (gross, before transaction costs the figure is even larger). Always attribute "Source: SEBI." Never round up or dramatize — the un-fakeability *is* the asset; one exaggeration kills it.
- **Hook:** free, UPI-native, lagged data (the SEBI-compliant default), risk warnings as a feature.
- **CTA:** *Learn free. Practice free. Risk nothing.*
- **vs the guru:** "Not a tip channel. Not a course you'll never finish. A simulator the regulator would approve of."

### D. B2B / Broker (one lighthouse logo)
- **Reality check:** this is a **Phase 2 narrative, not a Q1 motion.** Enterprise broker sales need a security review, an SLA, and a named account exec — none of which 3 people have bandwidth for while shipping the consumer product. Keep the deck warm and take one inbound pilot if it lands; do **not** build a sales pipeline yet.
- **Promise:** "The SEBI-compliant education and onboarding layer your new F&O users need — white-labeled, paper-only, per-MAU."
- **Emotion:** de-risking + retention.
- **Hook:** embeddable "Options You Can See" widget; broker keeps the account, we make the user competent before they trade.
- **CTA:** *Book a white-label pilot.*
- **Model:** the Sensibull→Zerodha blueprint, but education-led and broker-agnostic.

Names signal altitude of mastery, not feature lists. Ladder reads beginner → prosumer → quant.

| Tier | Name | Price | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free | **lazybull Free** | $0 forever | 14-chapter primer + capped paper trades + limited AI tokens. The funnel. |
| Entry | **Plus** | $19/mo | Unlimited paper + drag-to-build + full AI teacher. "Trade with the lights on." |
| Core | **Pro** | $39/mo | Charting + alerts + replay + advanced analytics. "The whole workbench." |
| India | **lazybull India** | ₹399 / ₹799 | UPI autopay. Top-of-funnel + dataset, **not** core ARR. |
| B2B | **lazybull for Brokers** | ~₹20/MAU (indicative) | White-label layer. Phase 2. |

**Cuts and flags:**
- **Killed the $59 "Power" tier.** A 3-person team should launch with *two* paid tiers, not three — three price points means three value stories to maintain and a confused funnel. Its headline feature ("27-bot consensus") is **unverified in the product** (the hero shows 6 Greek explainers, no bot ensemble) and depends on a live data feed we don't have. Fold any real multi-model feature into Pro later, or ship Power in Phase 2 once the data is real. Do not market a bot ensemble we can't demo.
- **Pricing is a hypothesis, not a fact.** $19/$39 mirrors OptionStrat ($39.99) / Tradytics ($49). We have zero willingness-to-pay data. Treat these as test prices; expect to move them after the first 100 paying users.
- **₹399/₹799 India tiers exist mainly to test whether *anyone* in India pays.** Default assumption: India is free-funnel + dataset, monetized later or via B2B — not direct subscription ARR.

**Naming rules:** master brand is always **lazybull** (lowercase, one word). Tiers are short English competence-verbs (Plus / Pro). No cute names ("Bull Mode," "Matador") — in a fraud-soaked category, restraint *is* the brand.

## 5. The Narrative That Makes This Trustworthy

The category's perception problem is the opportunity. Trading-edu = sketchy guru, fake P&L screenshot, "DM me." Our entire strategy is to be the structural opposite, visibly, on every screen.

**Origin story (lead with it).** Three students from Newton School of Technology in India built this. Shaurya hand-coded the Black-Scholes engine (0.4ms full-chain pricing, live in the product). Pratham ran 120+ user interviews before a line of copy was written. *(Both numbers must be true and checkable — if the interview count is approximate, say "100+." Founders who inflate their own origin story in an anti-fraud brand are dead on arrival.)* The credibility is a working pricing engine, not a track record we'd have to fake.

**Four trust signals, made physical:**
1. **Paper-only by default.** Hero already says "TRAINING WHEELS" + "PAPER $100K." Every guru wants you to fund an account today; we refuse to let you risk real money. The thing a scammer can never say.
2. **No signals, ever.** We never say "buy this." Any multi-model feature is framed as *"watch the models disagree and understand why the market is uncertain"* — pedagogy, not a tip. Also the SEBI fix (the regulator is hostile to predictive claims).
3. **"Source: Mock" honesty.** When data is synthetic or the ML service is down, we say so on screen. In a category built on fake numbers, labeling a synthetic number is a radical trust signal. **This is non-optional given we ship with mock/lagged data — it converts our biggest weakness into our loudest trust proof.**
4. **The regulator is on our side.** As SEBI throttles live F&O, a compliant paper simulator is where people get pushed. Persistent "educational simulation, not advice, paper only" banners are brand, not legal cost.

**Compliance is a real obligation, not just a vibe (the missing piece).** Before the India page goes live: (a) a one-page T&C + risk disclaimer reviewed by an actual Indian lawyer — budget for one consult, this is not optional for a finance-adjacent product; (b) confirm we are *education/simulation only* and registered as nothing that triggers SEBI investment-adviser rules; (c) no "advice," no "recommendations," no implied returns anywhere in copy. The brand dies if the compliance posture is theater.

**Why the demand is real:** retail options flow is structural and growing (US listed-options volume hit record highs in 2025; retail is a large and rising share). The 91% loss rate means *demand to understand before risking* is the largest unserved need in the category. Someone builds the visual compliant on-ramp; we've shipped a first version. First + trusted is the whole bet.
